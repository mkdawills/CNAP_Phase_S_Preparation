M.300 R&R Budget Form › Additional Instructions for Multi-project · p. M-62

Additional Instructions for Multi-project:

Developing a Multi-project Budget: The structure of a Multi-project application reflects where the work will be done and not necessarily the flow of funds. If most of the work for a particular component is done by a collaborating organization, then that organization can be set up as the lead organization for that component.

The main budget form for the component must reflect the Unique Entity Identifier (UEI) for the lead organization and "Project" for the Budget Type. If the applicant organization is responsible for a portion of the work for that component, but is not the lead organization, then their costs would be reflected on a Subaward Budget Form with the applicant organization UEI and Subaward / Consortium for the Budget Type. Subaward Budget Forms simply record budget data. They do not indicate that funds must flow through the lead organization for the component.

The UEI on each budget form is used to identify the budget data associated with each organization. When the UEI on the budget form is the same as the UEI on the Overall Component’s SF424 R&R form, the budget data is associated with the applicant organization. When the UEI is different, it is seen as belonging to a subaward.

For more information, refer to NIH's Frequently Asked Questions on Applying Electronically.

Overall Component: Most budget data is collected within the Other Components. Complete only the M.200 - SF 424 (R&R) Form, Estimated Project Funding section and the M.350 - PHS Additional Indirect Costs Form (if applicable). The PHS Additional Indirect Costs Form is used to detail the additional contribution to the applicant’s indirect (Facilities and Administrative [F&A]) costs base from each subaward organization that leads a component, up to the threshold allowable under the applicant’s negotiated indirect cost rate agreement. The PHS Additional Indirect Costs Form should not be used when all components are led by the applicant organization.

NOFO directions, by component

Items marked CNAP guidance are consistency checks, not NOFO rules.

High-level info for the budgets

Requirements

  • Five-year budgets. Every budget and justification covers the full project period of five years.
  • Total cap. All the budgets together must not exceed $1.5 million in direct costs per year. Consortium F&A (facilities and administrative) costs don't count toward the cap.

Restrictions

  • No budget in the Center (Overall). Its only budget information is the Estimated Project Funding on the SF424 cover; NIH builds a budget summary from the other components.
  • No foreign components. Funds can't go to a foreign site. An unfunded collaboration with investigators abroad is allowed only with NIH prior approval.
  • No clinical trials. Human subjects research that isn't a clinical trial is allowed.

Consistency checks (CNAP guidance, not NOFO rules)

  • Each cost traces back to the Research Strategy. Every budget line should match an activity the writer describes in that component's Research Strategy. A line with no matching activity is the most common thing to flag.

How reviewers judge every budget

  • Reviewers consider whether the budget and the requested period of support are fully justified and reasonable in relation to the proposed research.
Admin CoreWriter: Heather Bailey (with Morgan) · Who's budgeted: Heather Bailey (Center Director, PD/PI); Carl Ade (Associate Director)

Requirements

  • Center Director (PD/PI) effort. Heather Bailey, the Center Director (PD/PI), must commit at least 3 person-months of effort to running the Center and giving investigators career guidance.
  • NISBRE attendance. The Center Director (PD/PI) must attend the National IDeA Symposium of Biomedical Research Excellence (NISBRE), held every two years.

What the Admin Core can pay for

  • Advisory Committee fees for external members, paid as consultants or through a fee-for-service arrangement.
  • Mentors, up to 1 person-month of effort. Mentors from non-IDeA states can be paid as consultants or fee-for-service.
  • Faculty recruitment at any career stage within the Center's research area: salary, supplies, and/or equipment.
  • Alterations & Renovations (A&R), up to $300,000, to update research space used for the Center's work. A&R is requested in the Admin Core; Research Cores can't propose it.
  • Fee-for-service work at other U.S. organizations, such as learning new techniques, sample and data analysis, workshops, or scientific advising and mentoring.

Restrictions

  • Center Director (PD/PI) salary cap. No more than 6 person-months of salary support per year for the Center Director (PD/PI).
  • No research funds for the Center Director (PD/PI). The Center Director (PD/PI) can't use COBRE funds for their own lab's research, and can't receive research project support from this or any other COBRE, INBRE, or CTR award.
  • Internal Advisory Committee members can't be paid from the COBRE. Only external members are compensated.
  • A&R timing. A&R funds must be spent in the first two years (Years 1–2).
  • No funds to collaborators at non-IDeA or foreign sites, and no funds for collaboration there. Fee-for-service at other U.S. organizations is fine.

What the budget justification should explain

  • The Center Director's (PD/PI's) effort and salary request, and Carl Ade's role and effort as Associate Director (the NOFO sets no separate minimum for him).
  • Each Advisory Committee fee, mentor payment, and faculty recruitment cost.
  • Any A&R request: what space, what work, and how it will be spent in Years 1–2.
  • NISBRE travel for the Center Director (PD/PI).

Consistency checks (CNAP guidance, not NOFO rules)

  • NISBRE travel. Attendance is required, so look for travel to NISBRE in the Admin Core budget.
For reference: the NOFO's exact words

A minimum level of effort (LOE) of three person-months is required of each PD/PI to devote to administrative oversight of the Center and provide career guidance to investigators. A maximum of six person-months of annual salary support may be requested collectively for all PDs/PIs. A PD/PI may not use COBRE funds to supplement research activities for their laboratory or research team and is not eligible for research project support from this COBRE or any other COBRE, INBRE, or CTR award. Funds should be requested in the Administrative Core budget to:

  1. Compensate external members of the AC as consultants via a fee-for-service arrangement. Internal AC members may not receive compensation from the COBRE.
  2. Pay mentors for up to one person-month of effort. Mentors from non-IDeA states can be compensated as consultants or via a fee-for-service arrangement.
  3. Recruit faculty at any career stage within the Center's area of research who expand the breadth of the proposed program. These funds may be used for salary, supplies, and/or equipment costs.
  4. Up to $300,000 in Alteration and Renovation (A&R) funds may be requested to update research spaces associated with the work of the Center. A&R funds must be expended within the first two years of the project period.

Funds cannot be used by collaborators or for collaboration at organizations in non-IDeA states or at foreign sites. However, funds may be used at other U.S. organizations for fee-for-service activities, such as learning new techniques, carrying out sample and data analysis, attending workshops, or obtaining scientific advising/consulting/mentoring (as described above).

The PD(s)/PI(s) of the Centers funded through this NOFO must attend the National IDeA Symposium of Biomedical Research Excellence (NISBRE) biennial meeting.

Research Cores: BN, NI, and CNWriters: BN Core · Chip; NI Core · Doina; CN Core · Heather Bailey

Requirements

  • Detailed five-year budget for each core facility.
  • Existing cores only. No new cores may be established in Phase S, so each budget sustains, modernizes, or consolidates a core CNAP already has.

What a Research Core can pay for

  • Personnel and supplies.
  • New equipment and modernizing existing instruments.
  • Using, modifying, modernizing, or consolidating existing core resources needed for the Center's science.
  • Consolidation and sharing are encouraged. The NOFO strongly encourages consolidating existing cores and sharing facilities supported by other IDeA programs (INBRE, CTR, and other COBREs).

Restrictions

  • No Alterations & Renovations (A&R) in a Research Core budget. A&R can be requested only in the Admin Core.
  • No duplicate services. A core can't pay for services K-State already offers elsewhere.
  • No new cores. Establishing a new core facility is allowed only in Phase E.

Consistency checks (CNAP guidance, not NOFO rules)

  • Phase S framing. The NOFO describes Phase S as sustaining the infrastructure and consolidating cores, and asks applicants to modernize and consolidate core facilities. Budget lines should read as sustain, modernize, or consolidate. A line that grows a core isn't prohibited, but it should explain how it supports the core's sustainability.

What the budget justification should explain

  • Each person's role and effort, and the supplies the core needs.
  • Each equipment purchase or upgrade, and why the core needs it.
  • Operating and maintenance costs for all five years, consistent with the core's business plan and user fees described in its Research Strategy.

How reviewers judge a Research Core

  • Whether the core is well justified and supports the Center's science.
  • Whether the business plan allows for growth in the core's user base and for its sustainability.
  • Whether the Core Director can manage the core's resources, and whether K-State's institutional commitments will sustain the core.
For reference: the NOFO's exact words

The first two paragraphs below are budget text shared by Phase E and Phase S, so "establish Research Core facilities" applies to Phase E only. The last two paragraphs are the NOFO's Phase S–specific statements about cores.

Funds may be requested to establish Research Core facilities and/or to use, modify, or expand existing resources that are necessary for accomplishing the scientific goals of the Center. In addition to personnel and supply costs, the acquisition of new equipment and modernization of instrumentation may be proposed. Alteration and Renovation (A&R) of core facilities cannot be proposed under this component.

Core facilities should have a detailed budget for the entire proposed project period (five years).

No new cores may be established in the COBRE-S phase. The consolidation of existing Cores for technology advancement and/or administrative efficiency and the sharing of research facilities supported by other IDeA programs (e.g., INBRE, CTR, and other COBREs) is strongly encouraged.

For COBRE-S, the applicant organization must hold an active COBRE Phase 2 or COBRE-E award. Submissions should demonstrate how the applicant organization will further guide early career investigators towards independence and modernize and consolidate core facilities to enable the Center's research.

Scientific Research Program (SRP) Core (new)Writer: Heather Bailey (with Morgan) · Who's budgeted: estimated SI awards (SIs are selected after review, before the award)
Good to know: final SIs aren't chosen yet. The application lists only a pool of investigators who could become SIs. Before the award, the Center Director (PD/PI) selects SIs from CNAP's pool of eligible investigators and submits them to NIGMS for prior approval. SIs can't start work until NIGMS approves them. So the SRP Core budget is built on estimated SI award slots, not named people. Don't expect names, and don't expect project-level budgets: the application doesn't include detailed research plans for individual SI projects. No minimum number of SIs: the size of the SRP Core can change during the award to fit the Center's scientific needs.

Requirements

  • Detailed five-year budget for the SRP Core.
  • SI effort. Each SRP Investigator (SI) award must carry at least 6 person-months of effort each year.

What the SRP Core can pay for

  • SI awards (research support for each estimated SI award slot).
  • Research staff for SI projects: postdoctoral research associates, graduate and undergraduate research assistants, and other research assistants, when the justification ties them to the needs of SI projects.

Restrictions

  • No double IDeA support. An SI can't receive research support from another IDeA award at the same time (for example, INBRE, CTR, or another COBRE).

What the budget justification should explain

  • Effort and salary budgeted for each SI award slot (at least 6 person-months each).
  • Research-staff positions (postdocs, students, research assistants) budgeted for SI awards, and why SI projects need them.

Consistency checks (CNAP guidance, not NOFO rules)

  • SI award numbers. The SRP Core Research Strategy must describe the budget, duration, and estimated number of SI awards. If the budget justification gives those numbers too, confirm they agree.
For reference: the NOFO's exact words

The SRP should have a detailed budget for the entire 5-year project period.

Prior to award, the PI(s)/PD(s) will select the SIs from an existing pool of eligible investigators at the institution and then submit the SI selections to NIGMS for prior approval. The SRP is a required core of the COBRE, and there is no required minimum number of SIs. The size of the SRP may change over the course of the award to reflect the Center's scientific needs.

Detailed research plans with Specific Aims for individual projects should not be included in the application.

Individuals supported through the SRP must make a minimum level of effort commitment of six person-months annually. Salary support for postdoctoral research associates, graduate and undergraduate research assistants, and other research assistants may be requested with justification for the needs of the SI.
