Helpful notes
Page limits
  • Center Research Strategy: 12 pages for Sections A–E combined.
  • Admin Core and SRP Core Research Strategies: 6 pages each. CN Core Research Strategy: 6 pages.
  • Specific Aims: 1 page each. Project Summary/Abstract: 30 lines each. Project Narrative: 3 sentences (Center only).
The order of the work
  • Center Aims first. Your Center Specific Aims (Fri, Nov 6) go straight to the Core Directors, so every component's Aims (Fri, Nov 13) follow the same frame.
  • Strategies next. Center Research Strategy Sections A–E are due Nov 23 – Dec 4; the Admin Core, SRP Core, and CN Core Research Strategies are due Mon, Nov 30.
  • Budgets last. Admin Core, SRP Core, and CN Core Budgets and Budget Justifications are ready for Roxana's review Fri, Dec 4. The Center (Overall) has no budget of its own.
Reviews before submission
  • Budget review: Roxana first, then Tim in Central Office (Mon, Dec 14). Tim's comments are all back Fri, Jan 8; revised (final) budgets are ready for Roxana's review the morning of Fri, Jan 15.
  • Outside scientific review: every component's Aims and Research Strategy go to the external reviewer(s) Mon, Dec 7. Comments are due Jan 4; you and Morgan sort them Jan 6; revisions are due Mon, Jan 11.
  • Biosketch and Current & Pending review: drafts Ready for Review Thu, Dec 17; Kim's comments back Fri, Jan 8; finals due Wed, Jan 13.
Your role and the rules tied to it (NOFO)
  • Single-PD/PI application. As Center Director (PD/PI), you are the only person in the Center's (Overall's) Senior/Key profile. Carl Ade is listed in the Admin Core as Associate Director.
  • Effort and salary. At least 3 person-months of effort in the Admin Core; Center Director (PD/PI) salary support is capped at 6 person-months.
  • NISBRE. You must attend the biennial National IDeA Symposium of Biomedical Research Excellence; budget the travel in the Admin Core (CNAP).
  • SRP Investigators (SIs) are selected after submission. The application names only the pool of investigators who could become SIs (the Existing SRP Investigator Pool, with biosketches). Before the award, you select SIs from that pool and submit them to NIGMS for prior approval. The SRP Core budget uses estimated SI award slots; there's no minimum number of SIs.
Things that can sink the application (NOFO)
  • The Institutional Letter of Support is mandatory (Mon, Dec 7). Without it, the application is withdrawn before review. It's the only letter allowed.
  • Don't name or contact external Advisory Committee members before the Notice of Award.
Also with Morgan (CNAP)
  • Data Management and Sharing Plan: Center only; due Thu, Dec 17.
  • Appendix: optional and limited; tell Morgan by Fri, Dec 11 if any component needs one.
  • Admin items: confirm the external reviewer(s) Fri, Nov 20; answer the Cayuse human subjects, vertebrate animals, and other-attachment questions for every component Fri, Dec 18; decide on an optional cover letter by Tue, Jan 12.

A. Justification for the Center Mon, Nov 23

What to include

  • Make the case for the Center. Give a strong rationale for why K-State needs support for the Center in its scientific area, and show how the Center fits K-State's strategic plan.
  • Define a research area broad enough to grow. It should allow significant growth in research capacity while the components still hold together as one Center.
  • Describe K-State's research base. For example, give the size of the biomedical research faculty and the total external biomedical research and infrastructure grant support.
  • Describe existing infrastructure. Explain what research infrastructure K-State already has and how it will be directed to serve the Center's activities.
  • State K-State's commitments. Describe K-State's institutional commitments to sustaining research capacity in the Center's area (the Phase S version of this requirement).

How reviewers will score it

  • The Significance questions (listed under Center Specific Aims) and the Environment questions (listed under Equipment).

What to include

  • Explain each component's role. Describe how each component contributes to the Center's goals and how the components work together.
  • Show the cores don't duplicate. Explain how each Research Core fits with existing K-State resources, including equipment, instrumentation, and cores supported by current or prior IDeA awards (COBRE, INBRE, or CTR).

C. Sustainability Plan Wed, Dec 2

What to include

  • Outline the actions K-State will take to sustain the Center's resources.
  • Explain how scientific growth continues across the Center's whole research area once COBRE support ends.
  • Explain how you'll keep people and leadership. Describe strategies to retain Center faculty, and to keep leadership continuous by recruiting administrative leaders.
  • Optional: describe long-term collaborations with collaborators in non-IDeA states.

The Phase S focus (NOFO)

  • Phase S solidifies the research base built in earlier phases and carries out a Sustainability Plan to continue research excellence beyond COBRE funding.
  • Show how K-State will further guide early career investigators toward independence and modernize and consolidate core facilities.

How reviewers will score it

  • Is the Sustainability Plan consistent with the earlier expansion plans and with sustainability through Phase S and beyond?

D. Advisory Committee Thu, Dec 3

Required makeup

  • Three external, nationally recognized scientists with balanced expertise directly relevant to the Center's area.
  • One senior institutional official (such as a Dean or Vice President for Research) and one senior faculty member appointed by that official.
  • Meets at least twice a year. Members who served as external advisors before may continue.
  • No other Center role. An AC member can't hold any other position in the Center, including mentoring an SI.

What to describe

  • Describe the external members you want. Explain the qualities and scientific expertise you're looking for and how members will be selected, without naming anyone.
  • Explain how the AC works. Describe its function and operations, and any additional responsibilities you're proposing for it.

Restrictions

  • Don't name or contact external AC members before the Notice of Award.

How reviewers will score it

  • Is the AC's makeup appropriate? Is its expertise adequate to assess the Center's progress and advise on science and career development, including assessing SIs? Is its role clearly described?

E. Career Development Plan Fri, Dec 4

Required elements

  • Carried out by the SRP Core to support SI success, with leadership and management training built into the Center.
  • A mentor for each SI: an established investigator who can give scientific advice and career guidance. Mentors may come from another organization, including one in a non-IDeA state.

What to describe

  • Describe the training. List the structured activities that will build SIs' professional and leadership skills (for example, conflict resolution, lab management, balancing competing priorities, managing finances).
  • Explain how SIs advance. Describe strategies to promote and retain SIs, and the milestones you expect them to reach, such as submitting competitive grant applications.
  • Explain the mentoring system. Describe how mentors are recruited (including their qualifications), selected, assigned to SIs, trained in evidence-informed mentoring strategies, and assessed.
  • Plan for problems. Describe how you'll mentor SIs who miss milestones, and how conflicts between a mentor and an SI will be resolved.

How reviewers will score it

  • Are there clear strategies to prepare, train, and guide mentors? Is mentoring performance assessed? Is mentor–SI conflict resolution addressed?
  • Are there processes to evaluate SIs' progress in generating data, publishing, and securing independent funding?

NOFO, Sec. IV.2, Overall Component › PHS 398 Research Plan › Research Strategy › A. Justification for the Center

Applicants should provide a strong rationale for the need to request support for the Center in the chosen scientific area and how it fits within the applicant organization's strategic plan. The research area of the Center should be sufficiently broad to enable a significant expansion of biomedical research capacity while maintaining cohesion among its components. The justification should describe the organization's:

  1. Research base (the size of the biomedical research faculty, the total external biomedical research and infrastructure grant support, etc.),
  2. Existing institutional research infrastructure and how it will be directed to serve the Center's activities, and
  3. Institutional commitments to the expansion and growth (COBRE-E) or sustainability (COBRE-S) of biomedical research capacity in the chosen broad area of research
NOFO, Sec. IV.2, Overall Component › PHS 398 Research Plan › Research Strategy › B. Organization and Management Plan

The Organization and Management Plan for the Center should clearly convey a strategy that demonstrates an effective use of the available funding, the proposed configuration of the Center and its cores, and the approaches to expand (COBRE-E) or sustain (COBRE-S) biomedical research capacity. Describe:

  1. How each of the Center's components will contribute to the Center's goals and synergize with each other, and
  2. How the proposed Research Cores (if applicable) fit with existing resources at the organization without being duplicative; include equipment, instrumentation, and cores supported by current or prior IDeA (COBRE, INBRE, or CTR) awards.
NOFO, Sec. IV.2, Overall Component › PHS 398 Research Plan › Research Strategy › C. Sustainability Plan

The Sustainability Plan should outline the actions to be undertaken during the E-phase to ensure the enhancements to research infrastructure endure through the S-phase and beyond the duration of COBRE support. COBRE-S applicants should outline the actions the organization will take to successfully sustain the center's resources. Similarly, the plan should describe strategies to continue building sustained scientific growth across the entire research area of the Center once COBRE support ends. Strategies to promote the retention of center-associated faculty as well as plans to ensure continuity of leadership through the recruitment of administrative leaders should also be included. This could also include long-term collaborations between the IDeA awardee and applicable collaborators in non-IDeA states.

NOFO, Sec. I (Overall Plan)

The focus of the COBRE-S award is to solidify the research base built in previous COBRE Phases and to implement a Sustainability Plan to continue research excellence beyond COBRE funding.

For COBRE-S, the applicant organization must hold an active COBRE Phase 2 or COBRE-E award. Submissions should demonstrate how the applicant organization will further guide early career investigators towards independence and modernize and consolidate core facilities to enable the Center's research.

NOFO, Sec. IV.2, Overall Component › PHS 398 Research Plan › Research Strategy › D. Advisory Committee

An Advisory Committee (AC) composed of three external, nationally recognized scientists with balanced expertise directly relevant to the broad scientific area of the Center, one senior institutional official (such as a Dean or Vice-President for Research), and one senior faculty member appointed by the senior institutional official must be assembled to advise the PD(s)/PI(s). AC members who previously served as external advisors may continue to serve in this role. The AC should meet at least twice per year. An AC member may not hold any other position in the Center, including serving as a mentor to an SI.

Responsibilities of the AC include but are not limited to:

  1. Reviewing the annual Center evaluation provided by its Administrative Core
  2. Making recommendations to the PD(s)/PI(s) to improve the Center's performance
  3. Providing feedback to the SIs, their mentors, and the PD(s)/PI(s) regarding their progress in research and career development,
  4. Advising the PD(s)/PI(s) on ways to remediate concerns with SI productivity,
  5. Assessing research plans and making recommendations for the approval of replacement SIs when appropriate, and
  6. Providing feedback on the progress of the Sustainability Plan.

Plans for the AC should describe:

  1. The qualities and scientific expertise desired for external AC members and the process for their selection. External AC members should not be named nor contacted prior to receiving the Notice of Award (NoA) for the application,
  2. The function and operations of the AC, and
  3. Any additional responsibilities proposed for the AC.
NOFO, Sec. IV.2, Overall Component › PHS 398 Research Plan › Research Strategy › E. Career Development Plan

A Career Development Plan is required that will be implemented by the SRP to support the success of the SIs. Leadership and management training for the SIs should be infused into the Center to foster career and professional advancement. Additionally, as part of the career development efforts, SIs should each be assigned a mentor. The mentor should be an established investigator who is able to provide scientific advice and career guidance to the SI. If a suitable mentor at the applicant institution is not available, it is acceptable to enlist an appropriate mentor from another organization, including those located in non-IDeA states. The Career Development Plan should describe:

  1. Structured activities that will equip the SIs with professional and leadership skills (for example, emotional intelligence, conflict resolution, laboratory management, balancing competing priorities, managing finances, etc.) to be an effective leader,
  2. Overall strategies for promotion and retention of the SIs,
  3. Expectations for SIs achieving milestones such as submission of competitive grant applications,
  4. Plans for the recruitment of mentors (including their qualifications to provide scientific advice and career development guidance), selection and assignment to the SIs,
  5. Training of mentors to ensure the use of evidence-informed strategies that promote development of the SI,
  6. Assessment of mentors' performance,
  7. Mentoring strategies for SIs who fall short of meeting established milestones, and
  8. Resolution of conflicts between the mentor and the SI
Review criteria · NOFO, Sec. V.1, Scored Review Criteria - Overall › Environment

Will the scientific environment in which the work will be done contribute to the probability of success? Does the application demonstrate appropriate institutional commitment to support the goal of strengthening the biomedical research capacity in the scientific area of the Center? Are the existing physical resources available to the investigators adequate to meet the goals of the Center? Is the existing pool of potential SIs, as a whole, scientifically broad and well-integrated to spur research capacity growth in the proposed area of investigation? How effectively do the overall plans convey the need to request support to expand (COBRE-E) or sustain (COBRE-S) existing resources and future growth in the proposed broad area of research, as applicable?

Review criteria · NOFO, Sec. V.1, Scored Review Criteria - Overall › Approach

Does the Organization and Management Plan provide confidence that the overall goals of the Center will be achieved?

Is the Sustainability Plan congruent with the plans for expansion during the COBRE-E Phase and future sustainability through the COBRE-S Phase and beyond?

Is the proposed composition of the Advisory Committee (AC) appropriate? Are the scientific and administrative expertise of the AC members adequate to assess the research and capacity building progress of the Center and to offer advice to the PD(s)/PI(s) on scientific and career development matters, including the assessment of SIs and their research? Is the role of the AC in these matters adequately described?

Does the Career Development Plan include clear strategies to prepare, train, and guide mentors in their role? Is mentoring performance adequately assessed and evaluated? Does the application adequately address how conflicts that arise between the mentor and the SI will be resolved?

Are there processes proposed to evaluate the progress and success of the SIs for generating useful data, publishing their results, and securing peer-reviewed independent funding?

Review criteria · NOFO, Sec. V.1, Scored Review Criteria - Overall › Innovation

Will the proposed Center's area of science enable future growth on pace with advances in the field? Do the proposed plans demonstrate effective and creative use of existing resources and investigators across the applicant organization? To what extent will the plans set the institution and its investigators on a path towards innovative research in the broad scientific area of the Center?
