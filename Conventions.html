Formatting Conventions

  • Names and terms, NIH format, tables and figures, and references.
  • Consider referencing this while you write.
  • Items marked New or Updated are worth a closer look, even if you've written for CNAP before.

Names and terms
Convention
What to do
Example
1. Phases and this applicationUpdated
Capital P, then the number or letter: “Phase 1,” “Phase 2,” “Phase S.” This application is Phase S, the Sustainability phase (the NOFO's “COBRE-S”). Don't call it “Phase 3.”
“…established in Phase 1 and expanded in Phase 2, Phase S support will sustain…”
2. CNAP
Four letters, no dash.
CNAP (not C-NAP)
3. K-State
Use “K-State,” never “KSU.” Spell out “Kansas State University (K-State)” at first mention in each component.
“the K-State campus data management plan”
4. Core
Capitalize when naming one of the three research cores; lowercase otherwise.
“NI Core users rated core services…”
5. Names for the partsNew
  • Center: CNAP as a whole
  • Overall component: the application part only
  • Center Director (PD/PI): Heather Bailey's role. Write it this way, not "PD/PI" or "PI" alone.
  • Administrative Core
  • Research Core
  • Scientific Research Program (SRP) Core
  • SRP Investigators (SIs)
  • Advisory Committee (AC)
  • Organization and Management (O&M) Plan
“The Center's three Research Cores…”
6. AbbreviationsNew
Spell out at first use in every component, in both the Specific Aims and the Research Strategy. Reviewers may read only one component.
“the Neuroinformatics (NI) Core”

NIH format (required)
Convention
What to do
Example
7. FontNew
Arial, 11 pt or larger for all text. Text inside figures, graphs, and charts may be smaller if legible at 100%.
—
8. MarginsNew
At least 0.5 inch on all sides; nothing in the margins.
—
9. Headers and footersNew
None. NIH adds them. Headings within the text are fine.
—
10. Hyperlinks and URLsNew
None, unless the NOFO or form asks for them. Never put information reviewers need behind a link.
—

Tables and figures
Convention
What to do
Example
11. TablesUpdated
  • Arial 11 pt for table text and captions.
  • Black cell borders.
  • Caption below the table, left-aligned.
  • If a table won't fit at 11 pt, make it a figure.
Table 1. NI Core User Survey Responses — Quality of Services
12. Figure captionsUpdated
Arial 11 pt, below the figure, left-aligned.
Figure 1. Annual CNAP Beocat users, 2017–2026.
13. Bold labels
Bold the label (“Table 1.” / “Figure 1.”), not the text after it.
Table 2. Core usage by year
14. NumberingNew
Restart at Table 1 and Figure 1 in each component.
—
15. Inserting imagesUpdated
Insert figures as 300-dpi PNG or PDF-based images, not Enhanced Metafile (EMF can blur or shift fonts in the final PDF).
—
16. Placement
  • Half the page width or less: right-align, tight text wrap.
  • Wider: center, no wrap.
  • Check the final PDF; wrapping can shift.
—
17. In-text callouts
Bold the reference. “Fig.” for figures; “Table” in full.
“As shown in Fig. 1…” · “Table 1 summarizes…”

References
Convention
What to do
Example
18. Software
EndNote or similar reference software.
—
19. In-text citationsUpdated
Numbered superscripts, numbered in order of first appearance in your component.
“…growth in core use.1,2”
20. Reference list styleUpdated
A numbered style such as NLM (Vancouver). Not APA 7, which is author–date and alphabetical.
1. Author AA, Author BB. Title. Journal. 2024;12(3):45-67. PMCID: PMC1234567.
21. PMCIDNew
Include the PMCID for any cited paper covered by the NIH Public Access Policy.
PMCID: PMC1234567
22. One list per component
If your Aims have citations, temporarily combine the Aims and Research Strategy files so one list is numbered across both. The Bibliography is a separate attachment and doesn't count toward page limits.
