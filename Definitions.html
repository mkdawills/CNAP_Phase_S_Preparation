Definitions: Key Terms

Key terms as they are used in the COBRE-E/S NOFO (PAR-27-055).
Where the NOFO gives its own definition, that wording is followed closely.

Read the full NOFO (PAR-27-055) Phase S Overview & NOFO Comparisons
Program and phases
Term
Definition
IDeA (Institutional Development Award) program
The NIH program, authorized by Congress in 1993, that builds biomedical research capacity in states that have historically received low levels of NIH funding. Kansas is an IDeA-eligible state. IDeA includes COBRE, INBRE, and CTR awards.
COBRE (Center of Biomedical Research Excellence)
An IDeA program that supports the establishment and development of biomedical research centers in an area of strategic importance to the institution and aligned with NIH priorities. Each COBRE phase lasts five years.
COBRE tracks
D-E-S track: three phases, up to 15 years, for institutions with no existing research capacity in the area. E-S track: two phases, up to 10 years, for institutions with existing capacity that can be expanded. The COBRE-E/S NOFO (PAR-27-055) covers both the E and S phases.
COBRE-E (Expansion Phase)
Expands research capacity by growing the institutional faculty base, research projects, and core facilities. COBRE-E applications must include a Sustainability Plan to be implemented in COBRE-S.
COBRE-S (Sustainability Phase)
Sustains the expanded research infrastructure by continuing to grow the faculty and consolidating research cores, to maintain research excellence beyond COBRE support. Applicants must hold an active COBRE Phase 2 or COBRE-E award.
Limited competition
Only eligible organizations (here, those meeting IDeA, degree-granting, and COBRE-count criteria) may apply.
P30 (Center Core Grants)
The NIH activity code for this award (Center Core Grants).
Capacity and infrastructure
Term
Definition
Research capacity
The institution's overall ability to conduct competitive biomedical research in the Center's area: its faculty, research projects, core facilities, and supporting administration. COBRE-E expands it; COBRE-S sustains it.
Research base
As defined in the NOFO: the size of the biomedical research faculty and the total external biomedical research and infrastructure grant support. COBRE-S aims to solidify the research base built in earlier phases.
Faculty base
The institution's pool of faculty investigators working in the Center's scientific area. Both phases grow the faculty base through SI support and faculty recruitment.
Research projects
In practice, the SI-led projects supported through the SRP. Each is meant to generate data, publications, and an independent research grant.
Research infrastructure
The facilities, equipment, cores, and administrative systems that make research possible. COBRE awards enhance it in the Center's scientific area.
Institutional commitment
Concrete support the institution pledges for the Center's growth and sustainability (for example, space, positions, funds). It is documented in the single required Letter of Institutional Support and is scored under Environment.
Center components
Term
Definition
Overall component
The 12-page component that presents the Center as a whole: Justification for the Center, Organization and Management Plan, Sustainability Plan, Advisory Committee, and Career Development Plan. The DMS Plan also goes here.
Administrative Core
Required component that implements the Center's overall plan and manages operations: oversight, progress reports, compliance, budget, faculty recruitment, A&R coordination, evaluation strategy, and annual performance evaluations.
Scientific Research Program (SRP)
Required component that manages the Center's research activities and ensures the scientific and career development of its investigators. It coordinates mentoring, implements the Career Development Plan, and organizes Center-wide seminars, workshops, and retreats.
Research Core
Optional component (up to 3) providing shared technology, instrumentation, or services. In COBRE-S no new cores may be established; existing cores may be modernized and consolidated.
Core consolidation
Combining existing cores for technology advancement or administrative efficiency, and sharing facilities supported by other IDeA programs (such as INBRE, CTR, and other COBREs). Strongly encouraged in COBRE-S.
Advisory Committee (AC)
Required body (formerly the EAC) of three external, nationally recognized scientists, one senior institutional official, and one senior faculty member appointed by that official. Meets at least twice a year; members may hold no other Center role. Reviews evaluations, advises the PD/PI, gives SIs feedback, recommends approval of replacement SIs, and gives feedback on the Sustainability Plan.
People
Term
Definition
PD/PI (Program Director/Principal Investigator)
The Center's leader: an established biomedical scientist with administrative and mentoring experience, holding a primary appointment at the institution and at least one active, peer-reviewed, externally funded grant in the Center's area.
SRP Core Director
The leader of the SRP Core, listed as Senior/Key personnel (in the SRP's Senior/Key Person Profile, Project Role ‘Other’, Category ‘Program Leader’). SIs are not listed as Senior/Key personnel. CNAP's SRP Core Director has not been named yet.
SRP Investigator (SI)
A faculty member supported by the SRP who leads their own research program in the Center's area and receives COBRE research support and mentoring, with the goal of winning independent funding. SIs must be ESIs, NIs, qualifying mid-career, or “at-risk” investigators with an independent multi-year faculty appointment.
Existing SRP Investigator Pool
The institution's current investigators who could be selected as SIs. Each needs a biosketch in the Overall component.
Early Stage Investigator (ESI)
NIH term for a New Investigator who completed their terminal research degree or post-graduate clinical training (whichever is later) within the past 10 years. See the NIH Grants glossary for the full definition.
New Investigator (NI)
NIH term for an investigator who has not previously competed successfully as PD/PI for a substantial, independent NIH research grant. See the NIH Grants glossary.
Mid-career investigator
For SI eligibility: an investigator who has already won a first independent research project grant (RPG), such as an R01, and is working toward its first renewal. Should complement the Center's scientific breadth, and must submit a Renewal RPG application by the end of Year 2 of COBRE support.
“At-risk” investigator
For SI eligibility: an investigator who will have no substantial research grant funding in the upcoming fiscal year.
Mentor
An established investigator assigned to each SI to give scientific advice and career guidance. May come from another organization, including in non-IDeA states.
Plans and funding rules
Term
Definition
Sustainability Plan
Required Overall section. In COBRE-E it lays out how enhancements will endure through COBRE-S and beyond. In COBRE-S it outlines the actions the organization will take to sustain the Center's resources and continue scientific growth after COBRE support ends.
Organization and Management Plan
Required Overall section explaining how funding will be used, how the Center and its cores are configured, and how components contribute to the Center's goals and work together.
Career Development Plan
Required plan, implemented by the SRP, covering SI leadership and professional training, promotion and retention, milestones, mentor recruitment, training, and assessment, support for SIs who fall short, and mentor–SI conflict resolution.
Research Project Grant (RPG)
An independent, investigator-initiated, peer-reviewed research grant (such as an R01). SIs must submit an RPG application by the end of Year 2.
Independent research funding
Any substantial, independent research award from NIH or another agency, such as an R01 or R01-equivalent. Receiving it ends SI eligibility for COBRE support.
Person-months (PM)
A measure of effort: the share of an appointment devoted to a project, expressed in months. SIs must commit at least 6.0 PM; each PD/PI at least 3 PM to administration.
Prior approval vs. notification
Prior approval: NIGMS must approve before work begins (required for SIs with human subjects or vertebrate animals, submitted at least 30 days ahead). Notification: NIGMS is informed at least 14 days ahead; no approval needed.
Data Management and Sharing (DMS) Plan
Required NIH plan describing how scientific data will be managed and shared. Must be submitted in the Overall component on the NIH format page.
Alterations and Renovations (A&R)
Updates to research space associated with the Center's work. Up to $300,000 may be requested through the Administrative Core and must be spent in the first two years.
Clinical trial (NIH-defined)
A study in which people are prospectively assigned to an intervention to evaluate its effects on health outcomes. Not allowed under this NOFO; other human subjects research is allowed.
Review
Term
Definition
Overall impact score
Reviewers' assessment of how likely the Center is to have a sustained, powerful influence on its research field, considering all review criteria.
Scored review criteria
Significance, Investigator(s), Innovation, Approach, and Environment. Each receives its own score.
Programmatic priorities
Factors NIH weighs after peer review when making funding decisions, alongside merit, available funds, portfolio balance, and geographic distribution. They are not scored by peer reviewers.
