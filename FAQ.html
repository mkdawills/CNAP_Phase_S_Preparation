CNAP FAQ

Quick answers to common questions. Click a question to open the answer. Each answer links to the full details elsewhere on the hub.

Where do I save my drafts, and when?

In your component's WORKING DRAFTS folder in Dropbox. Once you're comfortable with others seeing your work, we invite you to save your draft there.

Note: your document is required to be saved in your component's WORKING DRAFTS folder no later than your document's Internal Due Date.

When is my document due?

Each document's Internal Due Date is listed on your individualized guidance page and on the Team Timeline.

The full application is due to Central Office on Fri, Jan 15, 2027. Central Office submits to NIH by Mon, Jan 25, 2027, 5:00 PM.

How do I update my status?

Open your individualized guidance page and find your writing assignments (look for the purple “Update your progress here” badge). Each assignment shows its current status on its row. Open the assignment, choose a status in the “Your status” box at the top, and it saves right away.

What do the five Progress statuses mean?
  • Not Started: work hasn't started yet.
  • In Progress: you're writing the document.
  • Ready for Review: your draft is ready for your reviewer.
  • Applying Reviewer Feedback: you're applying your reviewer's edits.
  • Finalized: Morgan and Heather have done the final review and moved the document to FINAL VERSIONS.

See Progress Status Definitions for who changes each status.

Do I send my draft to my reviewer myself?

No. When your draft is ready, update its Progress status to “Ready for Review” on your guidance page. Morgan and Heather will take responsibility for sending your draft to the assigned reviewer, and Morgan and Heather will send you the reviewer's comments.

If you have questions about a reviewer's comment, please don't hesitate to send your questions to Morgan and Heather. Morgan and Heather will gladly follow up with the reviewer.

Who moves my document to FINAL VERSIONS?

Morgan and Heather. Your assignment is considered final once Morgan and Heather have done a final review. Morgan and Heather will take responsibility for moving your document to the FINAL VERSIONS folder and for updating the Progress status to “Finalized.”

Please kindly refrain from moving your document to the FINAL VERSIONS folder or changing your document's Progress status yourself.

Does NIH provide a form for my document?

Stock format pages primarily only exist for biosketches and Current & Pending (Other) Support, which are built in SciENcv, and the Data Management and Sharing (DMS) Plan, which has an NIH format page to start from.

For other documents, write your document using the component's directions, and by following CNAP's Formatting Conventions.

What formatting should I follow?

CNAP's Formatting Conventions: names and terms, NIH format (required), tables and figures, and references.

What are the page limits?
  • Center Research Strategy: 12 pages (Sections A–E combined)
  • Each Core's Research Strategy: 6 pages (Admin, BN, NI, CN, SRP Core)
  • Specific Aims: 1 page (each component)
  • Project Summary: 30 lines (each component)

Your individual guidance page lists the limits that apply to you.

Where can I find the 2021 Phase 2 proposal material and 2025 Phase 3 proposal material?

These two Dropbox folders hold CNAP's earlier proposal materials, for reference only (not for saving new work):

  • 2021 Phase 2 materials: what CNAP proposed and wrote for its Phase 2 application.
  • 2025 Phase 3 materials: the drafts written last year for the COBRE Phase 3 (Transitional Centers) NOFO, PAR-23-216, which expired October 1, 2025. This year's application is for Phase S, under the new COBRE-E/S NOFO, PAR-27-055.

Phase S rules differ from both earlier NOFOs. Before reusing anything, please check that material against your component's directions and the Phase S Overview & NOFO Comparisons.

You can also open both folders from the Prior Proposals menu at the top of the Dashboard, the Quick access box, and the “Your links” box on your guidance page.

When can I expect email reminders?

Smartsheet sends reminders (from automation@app.smartsheet.com) at 8 AM:

  • One week before each Internal Due Date: a friendly heads-up.
  • Two days before each Internal Due Date: a gentle reminder. If that day falls on a weekend, the reminder arrives the Friday before.
  • The morning after a missed Internal Due Date: a gentle check-in. Morgan and Heather are also notified.

Reminders only go out for your own assignments, and they stop once your assignment is marked “Ready for Review” or later.

How do I add the Smartsheet deadlines to my own calendar?

Subscribe to the Smartsheet calendar. Your calendar will then show every deadline and update automatically when a date changes.

  • Outlook: Add calendar → Subscribe from web → paste the calendar link.
  • Google Calendar: Other calendars (+) → From URL → paste the calendar link.

Subscribed calendars refresh on their own schedule, so a date change in Smartsheet can take several hours to appear. Smartsheet is always the live version.

What's different about Phase S?

A few key changes: no new cores; the Scientific Research Program (SRP) is a required component; clinical trials are not allowed; and the DMS Plan goes in the Overall component.

For the full picture, see the Phase S Overview & NOFO Comparisons and the NIGMS FAQs.

Who do I contact with questions?

Morgan coordinates the full Phase S proposal: mkwills@ksu.edu. Please don't hesitate to reach out with questions, delays, or changes.
