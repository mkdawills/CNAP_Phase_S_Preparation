Review Criteria: COBRE-S Application

The questions reviewers will ask, first for each component and then for each general scored criterion.
Use them as a checklist while drafting.

Read the full NOFO (PAR-27-055) Phase S / NOFO Overview
Administrative Core
Is there a well-integrated plan to manage and coordinate the Center's components, Advisory Committee, and mentors to achieve the Center's goals?
Are there plans for the recruitment of new investigators consistent with the goals of strengthening and broadening biomedical research capacity at the Center?
Are there viable plans for maintaining a pool of investigators within the broad research scope of the Center?
Is there a plan to carry out the responsibilities of the COBRE award, including accurate and timely progress reports, compliance with federal regulations, and managing the Center's budget and financial reporting?
Are appropriate strategies described for conducting annual evaluations of the Center?
Are there suitable plans for implementing AC recommendations? Do the plans for Alterations and Renovations (if applicable) reflect a need within the scope of the area of research?
Research Cores
Is the request to support the Research Core well justified?
Does the Core provide suitable scientific support to the proposed scientific area of the COBRE?
Does the business plan account for future growth in the Core's user base, ensuring an expansion of its reach (COBRE-E) and its sustainability (COBRE-S), as applicable?
Does the Core Director have the expertise to oversee the maintenance and growth of existing core resources and laboratory facilities to carry out the application's scientific objectives?
Are there sufficient institutional commitments to ensure the resources and facilities required to sustain and enhance the Core will be available?
Scientific Research Program
How well-designed are the plans for the SRP to meet the goals of the Center?
Do the plans contribute adequately to the overall enhancement of biomedical research capacity at the applicant organization, in light of the chosen area of research?
How well do the plans align with the strategic priorities of the institution?
How well do the characteristics sought in future hires complement the existing pool of investigators?
Are the processes to solicit, review, and select SIs suitable, fair, and transparent?
How feasible are the plans for Center-wide scientific and career development activities in the Career Development Plan?
General criterion: Significance
Is the proposed area of the Center sufficiently broad and cohesive to enable a substantial expansion of biomedical research capacity at the applicant organization?
To what extent is the Center aligned with the institution's strategic priorities in developing research capacity?
How strong is the institutional justification for the expansion of the Center or the sustainability of the existing Center, as applicable?
Will the plans significantly expand and strengthen the institutional research capacity in the comprehensive area of research?
General criterion: Investigators
Does the PD/PI have the appropriate scientific and administrative expertise to provide leadership and direction to the Center?
Does the PD/PI demonstrate a commitment to mentor early-career and mid-career investigators toward scientific independence?
Does the PD/PI have a record of sustained leadership of a productive research group within the Center's scientific area?
Is there a strong and sustainable leadership structure for future administration of the program? If multi-PD/PI: is expertise complementary and integrated, and are the leadership approach, governance, and organizational structure appropriate?
General criterion: Innovation
Will the Center's area of science enable future growth on pace with advances in the field?
Do the plans demonstrate effective and creative use of existing resources and investigators across the applicant organization?
To what extent will the plans set the institution and its investigators on a path toward innovative research in the Center's broad scientific area?
Why the Sustainability Plan matters in COBRE-S. COBRE-S is the last phase of COBRE support, so NIH wants to see how the Center will keep operating and growing after the award ends. In the E-S track, the Sustainability Plan is first written during the Expansion phase, and COBRE-S is where that plan is put into action. Even so, the COBRE-S application must still include a Sustainability Plan section in the Overall Research Strategy, describing the specific actions the institution will take to sustain the Center's resources and to keep scientific growth going across the Center's research area once COBRE funding ends.
General criterion: Approach
Does the Organization and Management Plan provide confidence that the overall goals of the Center will be achieved?
Is the Sustainability Plan congruent with the plans for expansion during COBRE-E and future sustainability through COBRE-S and beyond?
Is the proposed composition of the Advisory Committee (AC) appropriate? Do members have adequate scientific and administrative expertise to assess the Center's progress and advise the PD/PI on scientific and career development matters, including assessment of SIs? Is the AC's role adequately described?
Does the Career Development Plan include clear strategies to prepare, train, and guide mentors in their role?
Is mentoring performance adequately assessed and evaluated?
Does the application address how conflicts that arise between the mentor and the SI will be resolved?
Are there processes proposed to evaluate the progress and success of the SIs in generating useful data, publishing results, and securing peer-reviewed independent funding?
General criterion: Environment
Will the scientific environment in which the work will be done contribute to the probability of success?
Does the application demonstrate appropriate institutional commitment to support the goal of strengthening the biomedical research capacity in the scientific area of the Center?
Are the existing physical resources available to the investigators adequate to meet the goals of the Center?
Is the existing pool of potential SIs, as a whole, scientifically broad and well-integrated to spur research capacity growth?
How effectively do the overall plans convey the need to request support to expand (COBRE-E) or sustain (COBRE-S) existing resources and future growth in the proposed area of research?
After peer review: funding considerations

These are not peer-review criteria. After peer review, NIH weighs scientific merit (from peer review), availability of funds, portfolio balance, geographic and institutional distribution, and relevance to NIH and NIGMS programmatic priorities:

  • Scientific breadth — scientific breadth of the Center and its relationship to institutional strategic goals
  • Institutional need — institutional need for biomedical research capacity growth in the proposed area of research
  • Investigator development — need for continued development of investigators in the Center's scientific area
  • ESI / NI inclusion — inclusion of Early Stage Investigators and New Investigators as SIs
