Scientific Research Program (SRP) Core

SRP Investigators (SIs) are the faculty whose research projects the Scientific Research Program (SRP) Core supports.
Everything below is drawn from PAR-27-055; section names in parentheses point to where the NOFO says it.

Read the full NOFO (PAR-27-055) Phase S Overview & NOFO Comparisons

The SRP Core Needs a Director

The SRP Core has its own Core Director, listed as key personnel.

SIs are not listed as key personnel.

In the SRP's Senior/Key Person Profile, the director is listed with Project Role ‘Other’ and Category ‘Program Leader’. (Section IV.2, Scientific Research Program)

SRP Core Director: not yet named.

NOFO text (PAR-27-055)

“The SRP Core Director should be listed as key personnel. Do not list SIs as key personnel.”

SRP Investigators (SIs)

Click a heading to open it.

How SIs are chosen
TopicDetail
At submission: the Existing SRP Investigator PoolThe application names a pool of investigators already appointed at K-State who could become SIs. For each one, include a Biographical Sketch Common Form and an NIH Biographical Supplement. The Personal Statement should be tailored to the COBRE and must not include Specific Aims or preliminary data. (Section IV, Other Attachments, Overall component)
Potential faculty hires round out the poolThe application should describe the characteristics sought in prospective hires. The Administrative Core budget may fund recruitment of faculty at any career stage who expand the breadth of the program. (Sections I and IV)
Before the Notice of Award: selection and NIGMS prior approvalThe PD/PI selects SIs from the pool and submits the selections to NIGMS for prior approval. SIs must not begin work until NIGMS approves them. (Section VI, Post-Award Requirements)
K-State vouches for qualificationsThe institution is responsible for establishing each prospective SI's qualifications. (Section III)
No set number of SIsThere is no required minimum, and the size of the SRP may change over the award to fit the Center's scientific needs. (Section VI)
Requirements every SI must meet
TopicDetail
Career stageMust fall into one of the four categories below.
AppointmentIndependent, multi-year faculty appointment (or equivalent at research institutes) at the applicant organization.
Research programLeads their own research program within the broad scientific area of the Center.
No other IDeA research supportAn SI cannot receive research support from any other IDeA award at the same time (for example, INBRE, CTR, or another COBRE). An SI may still serve as project lead on a supplement to an IDeA award.
Eligible career stages
TopicDetail
Early Stage Investigator (ESI)A PD/PI who has completed their terminal research degree or end of post-graduate clinical training, whichever date is later, within the past 10 years and who has not previously competed successfully as PD/PI for a substantial NIH independent research award. See NIH's list of grants a PD/PI can hold and still be considered an ESI.
New Investigator (NI)Has not previously competed successfully for substantial research grants from NIH.
Mid-career investigatorHas already won a first independent research project grant (RPG), such as an R01, and is working toward its first renewal. Should complement the Center's scientific breadth.
“At-risk” investigatorWill have no substantial research grant funding in the upcoming fiscal year.
While an SI is supported
TopicDetail
MentorEach SI should be assigned a mentor, an established investigator who can give scientific and career guidance. Mentors may come from non-IDeA states, and the Administrative Core budget may pay a mentor for up to 1 person-month. Advisory Committee members may not serve as SI mentors.
Career developmentThe SRP Core implements a required Career Development Plan.
EffortSIs must keep at least 6.0 person-months of effort for the whole time they're supported. Going below 6.0 needs NIGMS prior approval and will not be approved except in extraordinary circumstances.
BudgetThe SRP needs a detailed budget for the full 5-year period. Salary support for postdocs and graduate or undergraduate research assistants may be requested, with justification of the SI's needs.
Time limitSIs are expected to be supported for up to 3 years. Support beyond 3 years needs Advisory Committee approval and NIGMS prior approval.
Grant milestoneEach SI must submit an independent RPG application (New or Renewal, depending on career stage) by the end of their second year of COBRE support.
ProgressAn SI may be removed if Advisory Committee review shows they aren't making adequate progress.
When an SI leaves the program
TopicDetail
GraduatingAn SI is no longer eligible for COBRE support once they receive independent research funding. If that funding doesn't overlap with the COBRE project, up to 6 months may be allowed to close out the SI project.
DeparturesNo prior approval is needed when an SI leaves; report it in the next RPPR.
ReplacementsThe Advisory Committee recommends replacement SIs when appropriate.
Adding a new SI after the award
TopicDetail
Research without human subjects or vertebrate animalsOfficial notification to NIGMS, with documentation including Advisory Committee approval, at least 14 days before COBRE-supported research begins. No prior approval needed.
Research with human subjects or vertebrate animalsNIGMS prior approval, requested through the eRA Prior Approval module (NOT-OD-26-026) at least 30 days before COBRE-supported research begins.
What the request includesA face page signed by the signing official; project summary; research strategy (no Specific Aims); updated Current and Pending Support; a biographical sketch addressing COBRE impact; Advisory Committee approval; a letter from a senior institutional official; a Resource Sharing Plan; and authentication of key resources.
Human subjects research addsThe PHS Human Subjects form, IRB approval, and Human Subjects Education Certification. Study data must be entered in the Human Subjects System (HSS) before requesting approval.
Vertebrate animal research addsIACUC approval and a Vertebrate Animal Section.
Advisory Committee Chair's communicationEvaluates the proposed SI's research program, assesses how the SI will integrate with the Center, and affirms the research aligns with agency priorities and proposes no dangerous gain-of-function studies.
Institutional letterFrom a senior official, showing a clear commitment to support a multi-year faculty appointment for the proposed SI.
